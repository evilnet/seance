# Client features

How the parts of the client behave and why, moved out of `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-02. Each section names its source files, tests and browser check.

## Attention

**Attention** — `client/js/irc/presence.ts` (Vue-free, DOM-free): `IrcClient.setAttended(bool)` records whether the page is attended (unattended: hidden at once; visible-but-unfocused after `UNFOCUSED_AWAY_MS`) and `syncPresence` decides what goes out — on Auto `AWAY *` when it goes unattended and `AWAY` when it returns, unless `/away` is in force (`userAwayChanged`); the presence modes and do not disturb weigh in too (next bullet), and a page hidden while connecting declares it before `CAP END` (`registrationAwayLine`) or at registration (`presenceRegistered`). `pushMessage` schedules MARKREAD for the open channel only while attended, and attention returning marks it. `foreground.ts` feeds it from `visibilitychange`/`focus`/`blur` via `manager.setAttendedAll`. The server side is nefarious2's per-connection `AWAY *` (pre-away) and its webpush attention rule. Tests: `test/irc/presence.ts`.

## Increase Contrast and Reduce Motion

**Increase Contrast and Reduce Motion** — the stylesheet reads two attributes on `<html>`, `data-contrast="more"` and `data-reduce-motion`, never the media queries: `client/js/helpers/systemAccessibility.ts` (called first thing in `boot()`) sets them from `prefers-contrast`/`prefers-reduced-motion` in a browser and the iOS shell, live, and in the Android shell from `SystemAccessibility.kt`, which watches High contrast text, the Contrast setting and the animator scale, because Android's WebView answers the media queries with the values its process started with (Chromium's `WebViewObserveAccessibilityState` is off by default) and the keep-alive service holds that process for days. The Android rule is Chromium's (`fromAndroidSettings`, `test/helpers/systemAccessibility.ts`). Two blocks at the end of `style.css`: high contrast points every muted/faint text token (`--body-color-muted`, `--chat-fg-muted`, `--chat-fg-faint`, `--rail-fg-muted`) at the full text colour, outranking a theme's `:root`; reduced motion sets every animation and transition to 0.01ms with `!important` (end events still fire for Vue's `<Transition>`). The per-element `html[data-reduce-motion] …` rules elsewhere stay for their still looks; a JS reader subscribes with `onSystemAccessibility` (`LinkPreview.vue`). A new reduced-motion or contrast rule uses the attribute, not `@media`.

## Switches

**Switches** — in the native shells every checkbox is an on/off switch (`helpers/nativeSwitches.ts`: the `v-switch` directive, registered in `vue.ts`, marks it as it mounts; every `<input type="checkbox">` in a component carries it, and `test/tests/nativeSwitches.ts` fails one that does not): on iOS it carries WebKit's `switch` attribute (the native switch; the shell's iOS 26 minimum is past the 17.4 that brought it), sized 2×1.22em in `style.css`; on Android it gets `role="switch"` for TalkBack and `style.css` draws a Material switch under `html[data-platform="android"]` (track in `--button-color` when on, thumb in `--button-text-color-hover`). Everywhere, checkboxes and radios take `accent-color: var(--button-color)`.

## `client/js/pwa.ts`

**`client/js/pwa.ts`** — installed-app glue: registers `service-worker.js` (secure contexts only), captures `beforeinstallprompt` into `store.state.installPromptAvailable` (Settings → "Install … as an app"), consumes `window.launchQueue` so `web+irc:` links reuse the running window (manifest `launch_handler: focus-existing`), and flags a new build when a newly activated worker announces a build token other than the page's own (`client/js/build.ts`; Help offers a reload) — `checkForUpdate()` re-fetches the worker script from the foreground hooks and hourly, since browsers only look on a page load. The token (`resolveBuild` in `scripts/build-info.mts`) is a hash of version and commit, `dev` in a development build. `tools/pwa-check.mjs <url>` asks headless Chromium for its installability verdict; see `docs/resources/pwa.md`.

## Searching history

`Windows/SearchResults.vue` (`/search`, or the header's search button, which toggles it): a panel beside the chat (`RoutedChat.vue` shows both on `/chan-:id/search`), over it on a phone. The route's `?q=` is the only state, and the chips and `SearchFilterSheet.vue` edit that string (`client/js/searchQuery.ts`, which says what the syntax is). The server (`soju.im/search`, `irc/historysearch.ts`) takes one nick, one substring and a time window, newest first, and gives no total. Extra words, `-word`, `account:` and the kind of link are checked on the device against what came back, so a page can be thin or empty while older matches exist: the status line says how many are hidden and how far back the results reach, never a total, and "Search older" stays until a page adds nothing. Nothing from a search reaches a channel: no unread counts, no cursor, no query opened or logged. A server without the cap, or no connection, searches the messages loaded on this device under a banner, with the same filters. So does an empty answer, in any scope, since it may only be the server's: a scan that ran out, a conversation it does not store, case outside ASCII. Every-channel results also carry loaded lines of private conversations, which the server leaves out, marked on the row; paging goes on from the server's oldest row, never from one of those. Tapping a hit takes the chat beside it to the message, as a mention does (`docs/projects/inbox.md`), and the results stay: a hit in another conversation moves the route there with its scope spelt out in `q`, which asks nothing new. On a phone the panel tucks away (`js/searchPanel.ts`) until the header's magnifier brings it back. The ✕ (Cancel on a phone), Escape or the magnifier close it. Under the empty field are the last ten searches on this device (`helpers/searchRecents.ts`, `thelounge.search.recent`), kept on Enter or an opened hit, never on what typing searched by itself, with Clear. A hit in a conversation not joined says so in the results. Each hit's time carries its day as well as the day's header above it (`helpers/messageTime.ts` `datedTime`): Today, Yesterday, the weekday within the last week, then the date, on the reader's clock settings. Left out on purpose: oldest first (it would only flip the newest page), role filters (history keeps no roles), several chosen channels (one request each, ~2 s of fake lag), and ranking by relevance (only what is loaded could be ranked). Browser check: `tools/scenarios/history-search.mjs`. Typing searches once it pauses for 0.8 s, on a word of three letters or more with no filter half typed, one search at a time and at most one per 2 s, which is what a search costs in fake lag; Enter searches at once. nefarious2 scans up to 50000 rows for each, stalling every client meanwhile (~165 ms measured), so do not make this faster.

## Channel browser

`Windows/ChannelBrowser.vue` (`/browse/:uuid`; the sidebar's join form has a "Browse channels" button, and `/list` opens it): the network's channels from the server's `LIST`, searched and filtered on the device. One `LIST` is the whole answer and costs one command of fake lag, and nefarious2 paces its own output (`list_next_channels`), so the rows are kept per network (`irc/channelList.ts`, `store.state.channelLists`) and the window asks the server only on opening with nothing or a list older than 5 min, or on Refresh, which waits 30 s after an answer; typing never asks. A second ask while one is under way is refused (the server would abort the first). nefarious2's `LISTDELAY` ("you must wait N seconds", a server NOTICE followed by an empty 321/323) is shown as a countdown and the notice is swallowed; a refused argument (334 usage lines, from `/list bogus`) is shown with the server's text. While a fresh list loads the previous one stays on screen. Rows: name, people, topic, the whole row joins (or opens a channel already joined). A query that is one channel name with no match offers to start it. Chips: All, Featured, 10+ people, With a topic; sort by people or name; 150 rows at a time with Show more.

**Featured channels** reach the client by two routes, either or both. An ISUPPORT token, `FEATURED=#help,#lounge`, with or without a vendor prefix (`nefarious/FEATURED`; the spelling is not settled, `ISupport.featured` accepts both): names only, in the server's order, which arrive with 005 (before the MOTD, before 001 under `draft/extended-isupport`) and cost nothing (`parseFeaturedToken`). And the MOTD: a line of exactly two spaces, a channel name and, after two spaces or more or a `-`/`–`/`—`/`:` set off by spaces, a blurb (`  #help  Questions about the network`). One space or three is not a marker, so an indented paragraph or a banner's art is left alone (`parseFeaturedChannels`, after any `- ` an ircu-family server prefixes). The token orders the cards; the MOTD adds its blurbs and any channel the token left out (`mergeFeatured`; tests in `test/irc/channelList.ts`). A server that gives only its short MOTD on connect (ircu's `NODEFAULTMOTD`, nefarious2's default) keeps the marker lines back, so when nothing named a featured channel the first ask of a connection also sends one `MOTD`, whose answer is read and shown nowhere (`IrcClient.motdForFeatured`); when the token named them, that command is saved and the cards take the list's topics. They are cards at the top of the browser, with the list's count and topic when the list has them, and a ★ on their row. The old "Channel List" special window (TheLounge's) is gone; the ban, invite, except and ignore list windows remain, and open the first time they are shown (`showSpecial` announces them with `shouldOpen: true`; they used to appear in the sidebar only and open on the second ask, which read as the menu doing nothing). The header menu's "List all channels" is a link to the browser. Browser check: `tools/scenarios/channel-browser.mjs` (desktop and `--mobile`), against the dev ircd with `tmp/nefarious-dev/ircd.motd` mounted at `/home/nefarious/ircd/ircd.motd` for the cards.

## Last opened conversation

**Last opened conversation** — `Chat.vue` reports what it shows to `client/js/helpers/lastChannel.ts` (Vue-free, `test/helpers/lastChannel.ts`; `thelounge.state.lastChannel` = `{network uuid, target}`; channels and queries, never the lobby). On the next page load `socket-events/network.ts` opens it as soon as the network is announced (the autojoin channels are already placeholders) or waits in the lobby for its `join` (a query is reopened with `/query` on `network:status`); opening anything else first calls that off. None of that on a page the user opened on purpose — settings, help, the changelog, a network's edit form, search results (`router.ts` `onStandalonePage()`): a network announced there (boot's autoconnect) and its registration's `init` leave the view where it is, the networks come up in the sidebar and the remembered conversation waits for the user; the connect form is nowhere, so from there the view always moves. Browser check: `tools/scenarios/reload-on-settings.mjs`. A notification deep link (`helpers/pendingTarget.ts`) on the network wins and resolves the same way. `join.shouldOpen` is true for windows the user asked for (`/join` via `IrcClient.requestJoin`, `/query`; a `/whois` no longer opens a query — its summary follows the user, `docs/projects/reply-routing.md`); a join nobody asked for (a held session's restore, a forced join, an incoming query) never moves the view. `ChatInput.vue` focuses the input when a conversation opens unless `helpers/device.ts` `hasVirtualKeyboard()` says a touch-primary device would pop its on-screen keyboard; a click on the sidebar row of the conversation already open changes no route, so `ChannelWrapper.vue` asks for the caret with the `input:focus` eventbus event instead. The composer shows that it has the caret: `#form:focus-within` colours its top edge (accent in the Ink & Amber themes, the input focus green in `style.css`) and the caret takes the accent, since a caret alone is a thin blinking line that is easy to miss. Browser check: `tools/scenarios/input-focus.mjs` (every way into a conversation, also `--mobile`).

## Channel order

**Channel order** — a drag in the sidebar (`NetworkList.vue` → `sort.ts` `sortChannels`) stores the network's channel names under `thelounge.sort.channels` (`saved-networks.ts` `setChannelOrder`) and rewrites the saved network's autojoin string in that order. `IrcClient.createChannel` reads `channelOrder(uuid)` and places every channel by it — the autojoin placeholders, a held session's restore JOINs, `/join`, queries — remembered names first, then alphabetical; `applyStoredChannelOrder` (`network`/`init` events) is the same rule over what is already there. ## Touch and iOS

**Touch and iOS** — `client/js/helpers/viewport.ts` publishes the visual viewport as `--viewport-height` on touch-primary devices (iOS never shrinks the layout viewport for the keyboard; `body` and `#viewport` in `style.css` are sized and positioned from it). `router.ts` makes every `router.push` a `replace` so the history stays one deep and iOS's edge swipe has nothing to go back to; what used to mean "back" (Escape on Help, the Android shell's back button) calls `leavePage()` instead — never add a `router.push` call site expecting history. There is no exception, an open image included: `helpers/imageViewer.ts` takes back with a `CloseWatcher` where the browser has one (Chromium) and pushes no entry, since an entry pushed for it outlives the image as forward history; on iOS an edge swipe leaves an open image alone. On touch the viewer owns the whole gesture (`touch-action: none`; any finger anywhere pans or pinches, and no touch reaches the swipes on `<body>`) and only a still one-finger tap at the fitted size closes it; its `touchend` is cancelled so no compatibility mouse events follow. Browser check: `tools/scenarios/image-viewer-back.mjs`. Hover or long press is decided by the **last pointer**, not the device: `helpers/inputModality.ts` writes `data-input="touch"|"pointer"` on `<html>` from each real `pointerdown`/`pointermove` (`pointerType`; a synthetic event without one is ignored, and a pen counts as a mouse only while it hovers — its taps are a finger's on an iPad and a mouse's on a Surface, so they change nothing), starting from `hasVirtualKeyboard()`, and the toolbar's CSS (`:where(html[data-input=…])`, keeping the old specificities), `Message.vue`'s long press and `MessageActions.vue`'s Copy text (the text is unselectable exactly while the last pointer was a finger; `onInputKindChange` feeds one shared ref) read it — a touchscreen laptop whose browser calls its screen primary (`(hover: none)`) used to lose the hover toolbar to its trackpad while the long press listened only to a finger; browser check `tools/scenarios/touch-laptop-toolbar.mjs --touch-laptop` (a `browser-drive.mjs` flag), and the `--mobile` scenarios tap rather than click, since a mouse click now means a mouse. On touch the message action toolbar opens on a **long press** (`Message.vue` `actions-open`, 500 ms, one message at a time, a tap anywhere closes it) and the text of a message that has one (`has-actions` = `canAct`) is `user-select: none` there so the platform's own long press (a text selection) cannot race it — a row with no toolbar (topic, mode, notice, condensed join) stays selectable, so its first long press is the platform's; the toolbar's Copy text stands in for the quick case, the message whose toolbar is open is selectable again once the opening press has lifted (`select-armed` in the coarse-pointer rule — armed mid-press, the platform's detector would start a selection off the same held finger) so a second long press there is the platform's own selection — and the moment one exists (`helpers/touchSelection.ts`, `selection-live` on `.chat`) the toolbar stands down and the whole scrollback turns selectable so the handles can cross messages; a tap collapses the selection and puts the rule back. Presses on links, buttons and nicks are left to them; `Sidebar.vue`'s swipe stands down under a selection and inside text fields, and ends cleanly on `touchcancel`. The composer lets iOS's Return newline land and removes it (`ChatInput.vue` `onEnterKey`), since cancelling the keypress leaves the shift key stuck; the send button prevents `mousedown` so the keyboard stays up. Browser check: `tools/scenarios/message-actions-single.mjs --mobile`; the keyboard and swipe behaviour need a real device.

## Message action toolbar

**Message action toolbar** (`MessageActions.vue`, `#chat .msg-actions` in `style.css`) — three quick reactions (the newest single-emoji reactions used, `quickReactions()` in `helpers/reactionRecents.ts`, defaults before any; one tap reacts, a pressed one is taken off; every toolbar follows the same list through the `RECENTS_CHANGED` bus event), the smiley that opens the picker, a divider, then reply, copy (code, and the whole text on touch), edit, a divider and delete, every glyph a FontAwesome solid via `::before` (the Unicode ↩ ✕ it used to be drew differently on every OS, and a ✕ at the end of a bar reads as "close"). An action emits `done` and on touch `Message.vue` closes the toolbar on it (Copy excepted — it is showing "Copied"); the emoji catalog chunk is prefetched at idle by the first conversation opened (`Chat.vue` → `prefetchEmojiCatalog()`, `helpers/emoji.ts`), and the bar grows in over 100 ms (`pop-in`, shared with the picker; hover stays instant). The bar is one row, always: `@container chat` queries in `rem` drop the quick reactions one at a time at 21 / 18.5 / 16rem of pane and narrow every button to 1.75rem under 14rem — an own message's eight buttons are ~20.75rem and a 390px phone is 19.5rem at the large step. It floats **above** the row, bottom edge on the row's top padding, so it never covers the text it acts on; the first message in the log, with nothing above it, keeps it inside the row. `MessageList.vue` sets `selecting` on `.chat` while the primary button is down and the pointer has moved, and every toolbar stands down under it, so dragging a selection never pops one. The jump-to-recent arrow (`.scroll-down`, its size and inset are `--scroll-down-size`/`--scroll-down-inset` on `#chat`) has priority: it stacks above the toolbar, and while it is up (`:has(.scroll-down-shown)`) toolbars sit left of its column. Browser checks: `tools/scenarios/message-actions-toolbar.mjs` (hover, drag, arrow, glyph, quick buttons), `message-actions-single.mjs --mobile` (long press), `quick-reactions.mjs --mobile` (one-tap react, close on action, catalog prefetch) and `toolbar-select-hold.mjs --mobile` (second long press = native selection).

## The iOS status bar

**The iOS status bar** — a home-screen web app on iOS 26+ gets no say over the status bar from `theme-color`, from the manifest's `theme_color` or from `apple-mobile-web-app-status-bar-style`; all three are ignored. What iOS reads is the `background-color` of a **real, painted element** positioned `fixed` (or `sticky`) within 4px of the top of the page, at least 80% wide and tall enough — that is `#status-bar-tint`, a bare `<div>` first in `<body>` (`client/index.html`) styled in `style.css`. Height is the undocumented part and it is the whole ballgame: the community's iOS 26 figure is 3px, **4px was measurably ignored on iOS 27 and 6px is where sampling starts**, so the rule ships 8px. Find no element and iOS falls back to `<body>`, which from 26.2 also renders the page under a feathered blur reaching ~93pt down the screen — that blur is what soft-focuses the channel header, and nothing else removes it (`black-translucent`, `black`, `default`, `viewport-fit=cover` were all measured and all did nothing). Two more disqualifiers: a pseudo-element is never sampled, and neither is a fixed element carrying a `backdrop-filter`. **The element has to paint** — the community's iOS 26 notes have `visibility: hidden` still being sampled and on iOS 27 it is not (measured) — so it covers the first `--status-bar-tint-height` (8px) of the page. Over the chat that is header background and invisible; the sidebar puts its logo there, so `#sidebar` reserves the same variable. Anything else placed flush against the top of the page has to do likewise. **File uploads** — `client/js/upload.ts` (`Uploader`, Vue-free behind the `UploadHost` interface, `test/irc/upload.ts`) posts straight to the deploy's `uploads` endpoint from `config.json` — unless the network advertises its own host (`draft/FILEHOST` ISUPPORT → `IrcClient.filehostUrl()`, kept on `serverOptions.FILEHOST`): then `UploadHost.filehost()` wins and `uploadFilehost` does a `TOKEN GENERATE FILEHOST #chan` (`irc/authtoken.ts` `TokenRequests`, answered by `handlers/token.ts` / `FAIL TOKEN` in `standard-replies.ts`) followed by a raw `POST` with `Authorization: Bearer` expecting `201` + `Location`; `test/irc/authtoken.ts`. `upload.ts` must stay free of `irc/manager` (it pulls in `document`), so `ChatInput.vue` hands `clientForNetwork` to `upload.mounted`. ## Server password

**Server password** — an optional `serverPassword` on a saved network (`ConnectOptions`, `irc/types.ts`), sent as `PASS :<password>` after `CAP LS 302` and before `NICK`/`USER` (`IrcClient.onOpen`); nothing is sent when it is empty. It is for bouncers that predate client SASL: ZNC 1.9 and older answer a client with no PASS with `464` and wait (ZNC's form is `user/network:password`), and ZNC 1.10 takes SASL only with `saslplainauth` loaded. It is independent of SASL; both go out when both are set. It follows the SASL password's storage rules: on disk only with `rememberPassword` (`saved-networks.ts` `forStorage`), with `hasServerPassword` kept so `needsPassword()` sends a link or an autoconnect back to the connect form; stripped from a settings backup unless passwords are kept; never from a URL (`linkTarget.ts`). The push worker's own connection does not send it: none of the servers that need one offers a push Seance uses (`draft/webpush`); add it to the stash (`webpush.ts` `writeStash`) and `swIrcOpen` when one does. In the edit form the switch off clears it, and on with the field blank keeps the stored (or in-memory) one (`fromForm`, `toConnectOptions` → `undefined`, `IrcClient.applySettings`). A `464` before `001` stops the connection instead of retrying (`IrcClient.serverPasswordFailed`, `handlers/numerics.ts`): the lobby says whether a password was missing or refused, and the entry stops connecting by itself; after `001` a 464 (OPER) is an ordinary error. Tests: `test/irc/server-password.ts`, `test/irc/saved-networks.ts`, `test/helpers/settingsBackup.ts`.

## IRC over TCP (the app only)

A network can be reached over plain TCP or TLS instead of WebSocket, in the app shell only: a browser cannot open a raw socket (Direct Sockets is for Isolated Web Apps on managed ChromeOS). `ConnectOptions.transport: "tcp"` (absent = WebSocket; default ports 6667/6697, `saved-networks.ts` `defaultPort`) is set by the "Connection" select in `Connect.vue` and `NetworkForm.vue`, shown only inside a shell; `mergeOptions` lets the connect form switch a saved TCP network back, and `findMatching` keys on it. `WsTransport` keeps its whole policy and takes the socket through `TransportOptions.WebSocketImpl`, typed as `LineSocket` (the part of `WebSocket` it uses): `client.ts` `createTransport` hands it `shellSocket.ts` `ShellSocket`, bound to the endpoint, which calls the shell's `Socket` plugin (`shells/tauri/src-tauri/src/socket.rs`) through `IrcClientOptions.socketBridge` (`manager.ts` passes `window.seanceShell`). ShellSocket keeps one promise chain of shell calls per socket (open, the lines of a tick as one send, close), fires its own close on `close()`, and error-then-close on a failure with the shell's reason; a page token with every open lets the shell refuse an open from the page before a reload. Without a bridge (a browser) a TCP network refuses to connect (`connect.tcpNeedsApp`). The shell does framing (CRLF out; lines split, 16 KiB cap, UTF-8 else Latin-1 in), TLS (rustls on ring, Mozilla's roots from `webpki-roots`; no pinning, so a self-signed server is reachable without TLS only) and the limits a page-reachable raw socket needs: the Fetch spec's bad ports less the IRC ones, no empty line or CR/LF/NUL, only registration commands until the server's first line looks like IRC (a prefix or tags, or `PING`/`NOTICE`/`ERROR` with a parameter; else the socket closes), a 512-line queue, 10 s per write, 16 sockets and 30 opens a minute; a page load closes them after a 1 s grace for the `pagehide` QUIT. A failure the next dial would repeat (an untrusted certificate, a server that does not speak IRC) closes with `FINAL_CLOSE` (4001): ShellSocket marks that close `final` and `WsTransport` does not retry it. The page may call the shell's one command, `seance_call`, from the `main` window only (`build.rs` `AppManifest`, `capabilities/default.json`). STS policies are kept per transport (`sts.ts` `stsHost`: `tcp:host`), since one server's WebSocket and IRC TLS ports differ; a WebSocket policy does not upgrade a first plain-TCP dial. `disconnect.ts` gives TCP its own hints. ## Interface languages (i18n)

The interface in 24 languages, right to left included. The long form is `docs/resources/i18n.md` (the catalogs, the tools, the fill and sweep, RTL, the `qqx` rig).

**Every UI string goes through `t()`/`tCount()`** (`useI18n()` in components; core `t` for Vue-free modules; the worker's resolver in `service-worker.js`) — never raw literals in templates, and never translated fragments concatenated into a sentence: a key is a whole translatable phrase, and a value that sits inside one is a `{var}` placeholder (word order is the translator's, free). Register new keys in the pot with `tools/i18n/add.ts`, the `#.` context written for translators and MT. **`client/locales/messages.pot` is the only source of English copy** — no English literal anywhere else in `client/`, a CSS `content:` string included (a heading CSS draws takes a `data-*` attribute filled from t(), as the user list's group headings do) — and `tools/i18n/check.ts` (run inside `pnpm test:mocha`, standalone as `pnpm i18n:check`) fails when a call site and the pot disagree, or when an English msgid opens a parenthesis it never closes (`opensUnclosed`: a sentence cut around an element) — it reads a key quoted either way (`t('x')` and `t("x")`) and masks only a `.vue` file's `<script>` block, so an apostrophe in template prose is prose. Keys are stable identifiers: change the `msgid`, not the key, and run `npx tsx tools/i18n/merge.ts` so dependent `.po` files get their `#, fuzzy` marks — never hand-edit a `.po`'s keys. The build tracks the language list: `translation-languages.txt` at the repository root is the only copy of it (24 names for the beta — en, which the pot is, and 23 catalogs: de fr es it pt nl sv da pl cs sk hu ro ru uk tr ar th vi zh ja ko fil, 93–99 % of the pot's ~2,300 keys each, the rest left to English; the 22 that were unusable machine output were dropped and their catalogs archived, not deleted), and `tools/i18n/sync.ts` (run inside `i18n:compile`, standalone as `pnpm i18n:sync`) scaffolds a new target's `.po` and archives a removed target's to `client/locales/attic/`. A catalog is filled — by `tools/i18n/fill.ts` on the engine the route table places that language on (`--force-engine` puts a whole run on one), or, as the shipped catalogs mostly were, by translation agents writing `msgstr`s directly — and then swept: `tools/i18n/sweep.ts` empties a slot whose text `tools/i18n/quality.ts` `slotVerdict` judges — braces that do not match the source (`placeholder`), an answer that is its English source again (`unchanged`; not when the whole answer is `IDENTICAL_EVERYWHERE` words — IRC, TLS, WebGPU, Qwen3, OK… — and not a plural form that echoes English while a sibling form is translated, "{count} Byte"), `degenerate` repetition, `runaway-length`, a script the target does not write in (`EXPECTED_SCRIPTS`), a body with letters and none of the target's own script (`OWN_SCRIPTS`), or `padding` that no longer matches the source's (a msgid with a space at one end has it on purpose; the fill puts it back with `repad` rather than refusing the answer), a run of English inside an answer (`english`), or, in a catalog with a script of its own, a Latin token the source has no word for glued onto Han, kana, Hangul or Thai or carrying a non-ASCII letter (`stray-latin`: ja `変iaz`). The fill refuses what the sweep would empty (judging a plural form alone, it also refuses the echoing form the sweep keeps beside a translated sibling), and an empty slot is not a hole: `compile.ts` leaves the key out and English serves it. The `.po`/`.pot` catalogs are prettier-ignored (tool-shaped — a po writer owns their formatting). User content (nicks, channels, message bodies) and server text (numerics, `FAIL` payloads) **never** pass through `t()`: they render verbatim, `<bdi>`-wrapped inside translated frames, and an MT pipeline is never pointed at them. Text inputs that carry user-typed text get `dir="auto"`; new CSS uses logical properties (`margin-inline-start`, `text-align: start`, `inset-inline-start`), not `left`/`right` — under `[dir="rtl"]` the physical forms put the layout on the wrong side (a genuinely asymmetric glyph gets an explicit `[dir="rtl"]` mirror instead). Translator/MT fill-in workflow: `docs/resources/i18n.md`; RTL is shipped (`ar`) and verified through the dev-only `qqx` pseudo-locale, which doubles every string and flips the direction. **`qqx` is a development build's rig and nothing else.** The generated catalogs carry no build mode — `compile.ts` reads no `NODE_ENV`, and `client/js/i18n/available.ts` marks `qqx` `devOnly` while `client/locales/tags.json` simply lists it — and what hides it in a production build is the build's define of `process.env.NODE_ENV` (`DEV_I18N` in `client/js/i18n/core.ts`): the selector and `activate()`'s resolvable set filter through it, the production `import.meta.glob` of the catalogs (`client/js/i18n/index.ts`) excludes `qqx.json` and `tags.json`, so no chunk is emitted for either, `vite.config.mts` does not copy `qqx.json` to `public/locales/` in production, and `scripts/build-info.mts` reads the pre-paint list through `readAvailableLocales` (`tools/i18n/available-locales.ts`), which drops `DEV_ONLY_TAGS` in production — `js/prepaint.js` is baked at build time and can check nothing itself. So a bare `vite build` behaves correctly whatever mode compiled last, and a production build leaves the tree clean. Vite builds production unless told otherwise: a development build (the dynamic-label instrument, the `qqx` rig, the call-site table) is `NODE_ENV=development pnpm build`. **Numeric `{vars}` localize themselves** (locale digits/decimal separator, grouping off); a standalone rendered number goes through `formatNumber()`/`formatCompact()` (`client/js/i18n/numbers.ts` — the unread badge does). **Development builds warn on the console** for a missing key or an unknown `{var}` (once per key+locale; the warned set is `missingKeys()` in core.ts) — the runtime tripwire for dynamically composed keys `check.ts` cannot see; production compiles the check away and the console stays clean there, no configuration involved.

## UI sizing: rem, never px

**Size the UI in `rem`, never `px`.** `html { font-size }` is the one UI scale (`:root` in `style.css`; the font-size setting's steps are `html[data-font-size=…] { font-size: N% }`, `medium` = 100% = the browser default and the default step (it was `large`, 125%, until 2026-11; a profile that stored `large` keeps it), the ends run 62.5%–212.5% on purpose, `client/js/helpers/fontSize.ts`; the sidebar is `--sidebar-width`, half rate and capped to the screen; beside the chat the user can drag its edge to another width, kept in rem as `thelounge.state.sidebarWidth` and applied as `--sidebar-user-width`, 8–30rem, a double-click restoring the default — `SidebarResizer.vue`, `sidebarWidth.ts`, browser check `tools/scenarios/sidebar-resize.mjs`; the phone's drawer keeps the default), and everything — text, header/footer height, sidebar and user-list width, button padding, icon targets, menus — is `rem`/`em` off it so a step grows the whole interface together and the browser's own font-size preference is honoured. In the native shells the system's text size stands in for the step while the `matchSystemTextSize` setting is on (the default there; Settings → Appearance hides the slider meanwhile): `helpers/systemTextSize.ts` sets `html[data-system-text]` and `--system-text-scale`, the system's body size over the browser's 16px — on iOS from a hidden `-apple-system-body` probe watched by a `ResizeObserver` (Dynamic Type), on Android from `Configuration.fontScale` as body text (16sp) feels it — Android 14+ scales large text nonlinearly — reported live by the shell's `SystemAccessibility` (the WebView's own text zoom is pinned at 100% in `SeancePlugin.kt`: it scales glyphs but never rem lengths, so text outgrew its boxes) — and `style.css` makes the root `100%` × that — 1rem is the system's body text size, 17px at iOS's default and 16px at Android's — capped at the `huge` step with `min()`; the chat header's buttons are capped at 56px (`--header-button-size`) in a pane narrower than 24rem (`@container chat`) so all five still fit a phone there, and keep growing in a wider one. A new `font-size: 14px` or `height: 36px` in the chrome is a bug: it stays put while the rest scales. Media-query breakpoints stay in px (they cannot see the root size); what has to follow the scale asks a **container query** instead — `#chat` is an inline-size container named `chat`, and the message row's three modes are chosen by the pane's width in `ch` and `rem`: columns (time fixed in `ch` per clock setting, nick `flex: 0 1000 12ch` down to a 9ch floor and an ellipsis, text `flex: 1 1 30ch`; the nick gives way first and alike in every row, so the columns stay aligned; below `72.5ch + 2.5rem` of pane the nick column starts at 9ch rather than 12ch, so short nicks stop standing off from their time), the user list as an overlay instead of a side panel once the pane cannot hold the widest gutter, 30 characters of text and the list (`--userlist-width` cannot be read in the condition, so its `6rem + 84px` is spelled out), and the phone's inline flow once the columns alone do not fit — that block used to be the 479px media query. Both `@container chat` blocks sit after every column rule on purpose: same specificity, later wins. Browser check: `tools/scenarios/message-gutter.mjs` (`SEANCE_CLOCK=12h+s` etc.), which sweeps window widths at three font steps with the user list open. The slider itself stays in px (`Appearance.vue` `.font-size-slider`): the control that changes the scale must not change with it. A drag renders only the sample box under the slider (`fontSizeScale` in `helpers/fontSize.ts` must agree with the CSS steps); the page takes the step on release, so nothing moves under the pointer. Browser check: `tools/scenarios/font-size-setting.mjs`.
