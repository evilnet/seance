# Language identification on chat lines (2026-11-08)

`tools/translate-eval/lid.mts` on `lid-set.json`: 166 English chat lines
("lol", "uncanny", "I know what to do tho", "mmm tweaking", …) and 173 in 22
other languages, mostly short. The question is the reading pipeline's: is a
line placed in a language other than English when it is English (a line of an
English channel translated "from Polish" for nobody), and is a foreign line
placed in its own language.

| Detector                                                  | English placed elsewhere | Foreign placed right |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------- |
| franc + the function-word classifier + word list (before) | 7 / 166 (4.2%)           | 47%                  |
| fastText lid.176 alone, probability ≥ 0.7                 | 6 / 166 (3.6%)           | 73%                  |
| ELD (small) where it calls itself reliable                | 1 / 166 (0.6%)           | 66.5%                |
| **Both, under `decide` (shipped)**                        | **1 / 166 (0.6%)**       | **143 / 173 (82.7%)** |

The shipped rule (`client/js/translate/detect.ts` `decide`), after the script
check (kana, Hangul, Han, Thai, Hebrew, Greek, Bengali, Tamil):

1. fastText's best at 0.3 or more, ELD naming the same language;
2. a language ELD's table lacks (id gl ga cy sw af), fastText at 0.6 or
   more, English nowhere in fastText's top three;
3. ELD where it calls itself reliable, English nowhere in fastText's top
   three;
4. otherwise unplaced, and the reader leaves an unplaced line alone.

The English miss is "sus", Spanish to both. Two foreign lines are placed in
the wrong language ("не знаю" Ukrainian, "дякую" Bulgarian); the other 28
misses are left unplaced: one- and two-word greetings ("merci", "hola",
"ciao", "tack", "terima kasih"), which the toolbar's Translate still
translates. ELD's medium and large tables scored within a line of the small
one, which is a quarter of their size.

fastText was given the line lower-cased (it was trained on lower-cased text);
ELD the line as written.
