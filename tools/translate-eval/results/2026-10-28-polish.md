# The cleanup pass on the web weights (2026-10-28)

Why English → English stopped fixing "your going to love this" in the browser, and what the cleanup prompts became.

## Setup
- **Weights.** Offline replicas of the browser's own WebLLM `q4f16_1` builds (`tools/translate-eval/mlc-to-onnx.py` then `mlc-to-q4.py`: 4-bit everywhere, the vocabulary table and the tied output head included), beside the `onnx-community` ONNX builds (embedding and head in fp16).
- **Request.** The shipped cleanup request, bare (`Correct: <line>`), greedy. The browser's request was captured from a development build and is the same bare shape; replayed on the web-quant 4B it corrects.
- **Fixture.** 14 lines with a mistake (sound-alikes and misspellings) and 6 clean controls, which must come back unchanged ([fixture](2026-10-28-polish-fixture.json)):

## The 4B's coin flip
On the web-quant 4B the first token of the answer to "your going to love this" is "you" 50.4 % against "your" 47.3 % (ONNX: "you" ~100 %). Once "your" comes out the model copies the rest of the line. The browser sampled at temperature 0.1, which made it an echo about one time in three. With the examples as earlier turns the first token is "you" 98.8 %. Mistakes in the middle of a line ("their coming", "you're car") stay ~100 % corrected either way.

## What shipped
- 4B (`prompts/qwen3-4b.ts`): the three examples as earlier turns, English cleanup only.
- 1.7B (`prompt.ts`): the three examples in the system prompt, English cleanup only.
- Every cleanup decodes greedily (`engines/webllm.ts`, temperature 0).
- A cleanup that drops its question's mark gets it back (`outgoing.ts` `keepQuestionMark`); the web 1.7B's "…, wanna come" had been refused as an answered question.

## Limits
- One 20-line fixture, greedy, one run per prompt; only the turns variant had its margin measured.
- No browser run: WebGPU's fp16 kernels were not measured, so the browser's numbers are inferred from the probabilities.
- English only. Other languages keep the bare prompt.
- Still missed by every prompt: "your welcome" (the 4B gives "your" 100 %), "were going…", and "affect"/"effect".
